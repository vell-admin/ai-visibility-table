// what ships

6 assets. Shipped, not discussed.

Same six on either track. The track only decides what each one is made of. There are working sessions, but you're not buying sessions — you're buying the 6 assets, shipped.

Position

Website: the 30 questions your buyer asks an assistant, who gets named today, and the one axis an engine can tell you apart on. Listing: the category call, the 9 or 90 listings you're ranked against, and the axis you can win on. Either way it's a decision, not a fact — usually made fastest and never revisited.

Discovery

Website: the title, the first hundred words, and the one-sentence definition an engine can lift whole — written against what buyers type, not what your product team calls it. Listing: 3 keywords and 250 characters. That's the whole budget, allocated against real search and what competitors already own.

Evidence surface, earned

Website: dated proof, named customers, third-party records, and the pages elsewhere that already say your name — surfaced where a crawler can read them. Listing: reviews, Foundational Technical Review, Competencies, compliance records. None of it can be written, so we sequence it by what's genuinely reachable in 8 weeks.

Answerability

Website: llms.txt, agents.txt, structured data, and the page itself rewritten so the answer to the buyer's question is on it, in a form a machine can quote. Listing: rebuilt to answer a buyer requirements document rather than describe your product. Since 30 Nov 2025 a buyer can upload that document and let an AI do the comparing.

Placement

Website: one citable artifact — a table, a benchmark, a definition — and the three places it gets put so engines pick it up and attribute it to you. Listing: co-sell activation — ACE hygiene, opportunity routing, and the specific moves that get an AWS seller to engage instead of archive.

Qualification and the scoreboard

Website: the 30 questions frozen and run monthly, with who got named and what moved, in a sheet you own and keep running without me. Listing: who on your list has committed AWS spend worth burning down, and who you should stop pitching Marketplace to entirely. Half the value is the second list.

// what this isn't

You're not buying sessions. You're buying the 6 assets.

And you're not buying copy, because that's now free.

On 16 Jun 2026 AWS shipped AI-assisted listing generation, and every AI writing tool will draft your website for nothing. The writing got automated. The deciding did not, and the earning cannot be. Copy is the only part of a page a tool can generate, because it's the only part that requires knowing nothing outside the page. Every one of the 6 assets above requires knowing something your page doesn't contain: who you're compared to, what the buyer actually asks, and what proof the engine will believe.

Which track is mine?

If the page your buyer's AI reads before it names a vendor is your website, the website track. If it's your AWS Marketplace listing, the listing track. One track per sprint; it's set at intake and it decides what each asset is made of, not how many there are.

How many sessions are there?

Enough to ship the work — typically a weekly working session across the 8 weeks, plus async review between them. The sessions are the mechanism. The assets are the product.

Who does the work?

I do the position analysis, the question set, the discovery allocation, the competitive teardown and the answerability rebuild. You do the parts only you can do — approvals, the review asks, the proof I can't invent.

What's the founding rate?

The first 3 engagements run at $8,500 instead of $12,500, in exchange for a named case study and logo rights. It's a straight trade: I need proof, you need a discount.

What if I'd rather start smaller?

The Second Buyer Audit is $2,500 and 10 business days, reads either a page or a listing, and credits 100% toward this sprint if you book within 30 days. Most people start there. If you want to see the method before you pay anything, the table and the audit script are public.

What happens after 8 weeks?

You own everything, including the scoreboard. If you'd rather someone kept running it than hand it back, that's the Fractional Marketplace Lead retainer.

Is this for someone not listed yet?

On the listing track, no. Get listed first — that's BUILD, and Tackle, Suger, Clazar and Labra do it well. On the website track, you only need a site that exists and a buyer who asks an assistant before they ask you.

// how it runs

8 weeks, in 4 movements.

1
Weeks 1–2 · Position
I read your page the way your buyer's AI reads it, and the pages it names instead of you. On the website track that's the 30-question set run cold, with who got named. On the listing track it's the category call and the competitive teardown. If the position is wrong, everything downstream changes, so this goes first.
2
Weeks 3–4 · Discovery and answerability
The words get allocated — the title and first hundred words, or the 3 keywords and 250 characters. The page gets rebuilt to answer the question instead of describing the product, with the machine-readable files beside it on the website track. This is where the comparison starts finding you.
3
Weeks 5–6 · Evidence
The proof motion starts: reviews and the FTR or Competency path on the listing track; dated records, named customers and the third-party pages on the website track. Evidence takes the longest to land, so it starts before it looks urgent.
4
Weeks 7–8 · Placement and the scoreboard
The citable artifact gets placed, or the ACE routing gets fixed. Then the scoreboard: the 30 questions frozen and scheduled monthly, or the buyer list split into committed-spend accounts and accounts to stop pitching. You finish holding the instrument, not a report about it.
